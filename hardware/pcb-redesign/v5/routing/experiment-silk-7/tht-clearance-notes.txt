The exhaustive comparison examines 319 plated THT pads, 737 tracks and 11
vias; 233002 unrelated pad/item/layer combinations have copper on the same
layer. There are 505 combinations at or below 0.50 mm (383 B.Cu, 122 F.Cu;
all tracks), involving 223 distinct pads; 221 combinations are below 0.25 mm.
These are pair counts, not counts of independent defects. The 0.50 mm review
threshold is a screening choice, not a fabrication or soldering requirement.
The saved project's default and power net classes specify 0.20 mm clearance;
the measured minimum is only ~0.00166 mm above that value. A passing DRC is
not proof that these locations are comfortable for hand soldering.

Tracks very close to THT annular rings can increase solder-bridge risk if
mask registration, mask dam width, flux, excess solder or exposed copper are
unfavorable. Copper spacing is not the solder-mask opening or web width; this
measurement does not inspect mask, solder wicking, pad-to-pad spacing, actual
iron access, assembly sequence or manufacturing tolerances. Inspect both
faces and actual mask/gerbers, discuss mask capability with the fabricator,
and review/route wider relief around hand-soldered joints as appropriate.
Do not treat 0.50 mm as an established safe hand-soldering limit.

Method and reproducibility
--------------------------
From repository root, using the official KiCad image:

docker run --rm --network none -v "$PWD:/work:ro" \
  --entrypoint /usr/bin/python3 kicad/kicad:10.0.2 \
  /work/hardware/pcb-redesign/v5/routing/measure_tht_clearance.py \
  /work/hardware/pcb-redesign/v5/routing/experiment-silk-7/result.kicad_pcb \
  > hardware/pcb-redesign/v5/routing/experiment-silk-7/tht-clearance.json

The script loads the actual board, not a center-distance approximation.
It uses KiCad's layer-specific effective pad and track/via copper shapes
and SHAPE.GetClearance, with values returned in internal nanometres and
reported in mm. It includes arcs, tracks and vias, tests each common
flashed outer copper layer, and excludes only equal nonzero net codes. Net
code zero is not assumed to be a shared net. Overlap is reported as zero,
not a signed penetration depth. It requires a two-layer board, and ignores
zones, other pads, copper graphics and holes. The six NPTH mounting holes
on this board have drill equal to pad size and no copper flash; they are
not plated THT pads. This is a geometric measurement, not a replacement
for native DRC or an electrical, mask, assembly or fabrication assessment.
The minimum is calculated independently of the reporting threshold. Use
--threshold-mm 1 to include the closest via in the detailed output.

Run synthetic geometry tests and the board/report regression:

docker run --rm --network none -v "$PWD:/work:ro" \
  --entrypoint /usr/bin/python3 kicad/kicad:10.0.2 \
  /work/hardware/pcb-redesign/v5/routing/test_measure_tht_clearance.py

Floorplan and decoupling feasibility, not a completed redesign
------------------------------------------------------------
The existing pad-center report shows C7 to U9 at 70.19 mm supply and
74.01 mm to its nearest grounded pad, and C14 to U11 at 34.87 mm supply and
35.88 mm ground. The report selects the nearest same-net pad, which is not
necessarily the IC's actual ground supply pin.
On this trial C7 is centered at (45, 191), U9 at (20, 259), C14 at
(55, 226), U11 at (46, 260) mm. C5 is already near U9 and R15/JP2 are
near U11: those occupancies and the existing traces make a simple move
without a new placement and reroute unjustified. Earlier closer placement
attempts did not complete routing (see ../README.txt). A feasible next
experiment is to reserve space for each capacitor beside its actual supply
and ground pins, rearrange nearby parts as needed, then reroute and check
the full supply/return loop, connectivity, sections, DRC and assembly
envelopes. Center distances alone do not prove loop quality. No new
placement or rerouting success, mechanical clearance, RF compliance, or
electrical sign-off is claimed here.
